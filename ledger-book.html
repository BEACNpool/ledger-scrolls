This exact cover is generated in your browser and minted on-chain with the NFT — what you see is what the chain keeps.

Who owns this book?

  • The NFT arrives in your wallet — you hold the book.
  • Whoever holds the NFT is the keeper: every future entry pays its ~1 ADA anchor to the wallet where the NFT lives.
  • If the book moves wallets, the entries already in it stay attached and travel with it.
  • The minting window expires about an hour after construction. After expiry, a verified supply of one cannot increase.

      A Book has a life of its own.

      Give it a purpose

      A personal record, project log, or guestbook for an occasion. The NFT identifies your Book; its public entries stay with its history when it changes hands.

      Let people add an entry

      Each entry records a name and message in a wallet transaction. It pays a minimum-output anchor to the current keeper plus a network fee. Entries are public.

      Connect an original, if useful

      A new Book can name an exact original from Ledger Scrolls. Readers can check that file beside the entries. A Book is complete without an attachment.

      Ownership, cost, and what the evidence proves

      A book, a keeper, a history

      Find a Book by $handle, holder address, or NFT reference. Its current holder is the keeper. The reader checks the native mint policy, reported supply, and available ownership history. Unresolved history stays visibly partial.

      Public by design

      Project logs and personal books use the same public entry rules. They are not private notebooks or owner-only feeds. Each entry pays the keeper an anchor plus a network fee; payment does not establish truth or prevent spam.

      Evidence, with limits

      Names are self-declared. An input address is not verified human identity. The app relies on providers for chain facts; it does not independently verify Cardano consensus or inclusion proofs. Read the shared guide ↗

      Beyond this website

      The open format allows other readers to recover Books. Long-term access depends on Cardano, retained history, reachable providers, and working readers. Saved links stay on this device.
